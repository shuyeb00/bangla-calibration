\section{Label-Efficient Shift Triage}
\label{sec:shift-triage}

The regimes call for different actions: nothing, a bias correction, a different system, or no prediction at all. Only
the presence of a shift can be detected without labels. We therefore turn the findings into a procedure that spends a
small number of target labels on the decisions that need them, and state what it guarantees. Proofs are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs} and simulation checks in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-simulation}.

\begin{proposition}[No post-hoc map repairs an uninformative score]
\label{prop:impossibility}
Let $S = s(X)$ be the score of a fixed model and $g$ any measurable map from scores to the probability simplex. Then
the target log-loss satisfies $\mathbb{E}_Q[-\log g(S)_Y] \ge H_Q(Y \mid S) = H_Q(Y) - I_Q(Y; S)$. If $Y$ and $S$ are
independent under $Q$, no post-hoc map beats the constant predictor $\pi_Q$, and for any classifier $h(S)$ and any
acceptance event $A \in \sigma(S)$ with $Q(A) > 0$ the selective error is at least $1 - \max_k \pi_Q(k)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Shrinkage versus maximum likelihood]
\label{prop:shrinkage}
For a scalar parameter in a locally quadratic approximation with per-item Fisher information $\tau^{-2}$ and prior variance $\sigma^2$, the MAP
estimate is $w_n \hat\theta_n + (1 - w_n)\theta_P$ with $w_n = n\sigma^2 / (n\sigma^2 + \tau^2)$, and it has lower mean
squared error than the maximum-likelihood estimate $\hat\theta_n$ if and only if $\delta^2 < 2\sigma^2 + \tau^2/n$, where
$\delta$ is the distance between the target optimum and the source estimate $\theta_P$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Ranking-survival test]
\label{prop:survival}
For a score that is not fitted on the $n$ labelled target items, the Monte Carlo permutation p-value of the AUROC with the
add-one correction is valid for the null hypothesis of no association between score and label, at every $n$. For $K = 2$
and $m = \min(n_1, n_0)$ labelled items in the smaller class, $\Pr(A \ge \hat A - \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/(2m)}) \ge 1 -
\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Coverage and risk control after selection]
\label{prop:selection}
Let $M$ candidate systems have fixed nonconformity scores. If each candidate's conformal set is calibrated on the same
$n$ exchangeable items at level $\alpha/M$, the set of any data-dependent choice covers a new item with probability at
least $1 - \alpha$; the same holds for conformal risk control of a loss in $[0, 1]$. For the false-negative rate,
calibrated on the positive items, a threshold that does not flag every item requires at least $M/\alpha - 1$ labelled
positives. If the choice and the calibration use disjoint parts of the labelled items, the guarantees hold at level
$\alpha$ conditionally on the choice.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility} is consistent with the failure of recalibration and abstention on OffMix-3L
(Section~\ref{sec:results-systems}), and Proposition~\ref{prop:shrinkage} with shrinkage beating maximum likelihood on
dialects, where the target optimum is near the source estimate; under collapse the optimum is unbounded, the quadratic
approximation does not apply, and shrinkage loses.

\paragraph{Procedure.}
(1) Detect shift without labels with a character n-gram domain classifier trained to separate unlabeled source and
target inputs; if it does, request $n$ randomly chosen target labels. (2) Test each candidate system, the fine-tuned
encoder and an LLM, for a ranking above chance (Proposition~\ref{prop:survival}, Holm over candidates at
$\delta = 0.05$). (3) If no candidate passes, abstain: the labels give no evidence that either score is informative,
and if neither is, by Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility} no recalibration, conformal set or confidence threshold is
better than ignoring the input. With few labels this also abstains on weak but real rankings. (4) Otherwise choose the
passing candidate with the lowest log-loss on the labelled items after MAP BCTS and predict with its MAP BCTS
probabilities. (5) Certify a fixed score. In the \emph{Bonferroni} variant the conformal sets and the FNR threshold use
the candidate's source-TS score on all $n$ items at level $\alpha/M$. In the \emph{split} variant steps 2--4 use one
half of the items, and the sets use the resulting MAP BCTS score on the other half at level $\alpha$. The guarantees of
Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} hold for any data-dependent choice, so the in-sample log-loss of step 4 affects the
quality of the choice but not their validity; for $K = 2$ the Bonferroni threshold on the source-TS score flags the
same items as a threshold on any monotone recalibration of it.

Passing step 2 shows that a score carries information about the label, not that it is good enough to deploy. The lower
bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} could set a minimum AUROC, but with 12 items per class its half-width is 0.353.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
We run the procedure on every shifted condition with a target calibration split, for every converged encoder run paired
with each general LLM and 20 resamples of $n \in \{25, 50, 100\}$ target labels. It is compared with the encoder
calibrated on the source, the encoder with the same labels, the LLM with the same labels, a router that reuses the labels
for choice and calibration without correction, and an oracle that picks the system with the lower test NLL (Table~\ref{tab:shift-triage-policies} in the appendix). With $n = 50$ on the
original shifts, the Bonferroni variant reaches macro-F1 0.794 (oracle 0.796, reuse router 0.788, always-LLM 0.715,
encoder with labels 0.682, always-encoder 0.658) and lets test coverage fall below 0.85 in only 0.006 of deployments (reuse router 0.124,
always-encoder 0.472). Most of that gap is the nominal level, not selection: Bonferroni calibrates at $\alpha/2$, and even a
valid set at $\alpha$ falls below 0.85 with probability 0.112 on 50 items (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-triage-tables}). On the held-out natural offensive targets, where the LLM is almost always the better system,
always-LLM is as accurate as the oracle (0.792) and the Bonferroni variant reaches 0.787 in exchange for coverage that
falls below 0.85 in 0.004 of deployments instead of 0.081: on these targets the procedure buys a guarantee, not
accuracy. Abstention here comes only from low power: no target in this study defeats both systems, so that branch is
untested on a true collapse of both.

The guarantees have a price that the numbers make explicit. Bonferroni-valid FNR control is conservative: on the
original shifts it raises the FPR from 0.324 for the oracle to 0.543. With $n = 25$ and about twelve offensive items it
flags every item, as Proposition~\ref{prop:selection} predicts. The split variant pays for its conditional guarantee
with abstention, which reaches 0.34 of resamples on the natural offensive targets at $n = 25$.

The label requirement is therefore a number of positives, not of items: 19 for two candidates at $\alpha = 0.1$. On a
balanced target 50 random labels contain them with probability 0.97; at the 34\% toxic share of MixTox-SA only 0.33, and
there the Bonferroni variant flags nearly everything (FPR 0.947); at 10\% prevalence 50 labels contain five positives on
average and about 245 labels are needed for probability 0.9. A stress test that resamples the natural offensive targets
to a fixed prevalence confirms this (Table~\ref{tab:triage-prevalence-stress} in the appendix). At 10\% prevalence the
threshold flags every item in every deployment with 50 labels, in 0.35 of deployments with 200 and in none with 400; with
400 labels the FNR is 0.034 at an FPR of 0.558, and on average over deployments 18.5\% of flagged posts are offensive. The point predictions need far
fewer labels: macro-F1 is 0.723 with 50 labels and 0.728 with 400. On a skewed target, therefore, about 50 labels
suffice to choose a system and correct its threshold, and several hundred are needed to certify it.
