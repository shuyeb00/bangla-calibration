\section{Label-Budgeted Shift Triage}
\label{sec:shift-triage}

The diagnosis calls for different actions in different pairs: nothing, a bias correction, a different system, or no
prediction. Which action a pair needs cannot be read off unlabelled data, and the regime labels of
Section~\ref{sec:background-regimes} need target labels. This section turns the findings into a procedure that
spends a stated number of target labels on the decisions that need them, and says what it guarantees.

\subsection{What can be detected without labels}
\label{sec:triage-detection}

A character n-gram domain classifier separates in-domain from target items with AUROC 0.982--1.000 on every original
condition. The models' own uncertainty does not reveal the shift: as a shift detector the encoders' confidence reaches
an AUROC of 0.504--0.686 on average, and the language models' uncertainty is inverted on tri-lingual code-mixing (0.297
offensive, 0.291 sentiment), meaning that they are more confident on the shifted inputs. Under collapse, abstaining on
low confidence gives no protection: on OffMix-3L the encoders' selective risk at 80\% coverage is 0.476, against an
overall error of 0.475. Detection is therefore easy, but it says only that the distribution has changed, not what to
do about it.

The candidate systems differ in where their ranking survives. On identical items (Figure~\ref{fig:encoder-llm}),
zero-shot language models rank OffMix-3L at 0.857--0.941 against 0.481 for the BD-SHS encoders, Banglish at
0.886--0.970 against 0.773, MixTox-SA at 0.795--0.831 against 0.687, and Hinglish at 0.843--0.890 against 0.735. In
domain and on the dialects the encoders are better (BD-SHS test 0.970 against 0.758--0.854). A guided-completion test
found no near-verbatim completion of any test set, and Llama-3.1-8B, which shows no completion signal at all, ranks
OffMix-3L as well as the other models (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-contamination}). The language models' rankings come
with a shifted threshold of their own: on OffMix-3L, MAP BCTS raises zero-shot macro-F1 from 0.470 to 0.752 with 25
labels and 0.807 with 50. Fine-tuning the encoder on the target labels cures the collapse only with about 200 labels
(AUROC 0.505, 0.621, 0.743 and 0.894 with 25, 50, 100 and 200; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-few-label}), so with up to
100 labels the better use of them is to route to a system whose ranking survives and to correct its bias.

\subsection{Guarantees}
\label{sec:triage-guarantees}

Four short propositions state what the procedure can promise. Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-proofs} and
simulation checks in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-simulation}.

\begin{proposition}[No post-hoc map repairs an uninformative score]
\label{prop:impossibility}
Let $S = s(X)$ be the score of a fixed model and $g$ any measurable map from scores to the probability simplex. Then
the target log-loss satisfies $\mathbb{E}_Q[-\log g(S)_Y] \ge H_Q(Y \mid S) = H_Q(Y) - I_Q(Y; S)$. If $Y$ and $S$ are
independent under $Q$, no post-hoc map beats the constant predictor $\pi_Q$, and for any classifier $h(S)$ and any
acceptance event $A \in \sigma(S)$ with $Q(A) > 0$ the selective error is at least $1 - \max_k \pi_Q(k)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Shrinkage versus maximum likelihood]
\label{prop:shrinkage}
For a scalar parameter in a locally quadratic approximation with per-item Fisher information $\tau^{-2}$ and prior
variance $\sigma^2$, the MAP estimate is $w_n \hat\theta_n + (1 - w_n)\theta_P$ with
$w_n = n\sigma^2 / (n\sigma^2 + \tau^2)$, and it has lower mean squared error than the maximum-likelihood estimate
$\hat\theta_n$ if and only if $\delta^2 < 2\sigma^2 + \tau^2/n$, where $\delta$ is the distance between the target
optimum and the source estimate $\theta_P$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Ranking tests]
\label{prop:survival}
For a score that is not fitted on the $n$ labelled target items, (a) the Monte Carlo permutation p-value of the AUROC
with the add-one correction is valid for the null hypothesis that score and label are independent, at every $n$; and
(b) for $K = 2$ and $m = \min(n_1, n_0)$ labelled items in the smaller class,
$\Pr(A \ge \hat A - \sqrt{\log(1/\delta)/(2m)}) \ge 1 - \delta$, so rejecting $A \le a_0$ when this lower bound exceeds
$a_0$ is a level-$\delta$ test.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Coverage and risk control after selection]
\label{prop:selection}
Let $M$ candidate systems have fixed nonconformity scores. If each candidate's conformal set is calibrated on the same
$n$ exchangeable items at level $\alpha/M$, the set of any data-dependent choice covers a new item with probability at
least $1 - \alpha$; the same holds for conformal risk control of a loss in $[0, 1]$. For the false-negative rate,
calibrated on the positive items, a threshold that does not flag every item requires at least $M/\alpha - 1$ labelled
positives. If the choice and the calibration use disjoint parts of the labelled items, the guarantees hold at level
$\alpha$ conditionally on the choice.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility} is consistent with the failure of recalibration and abstention on OffMix-3L, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:shrinkage} with shrinkage beating maximum likelihood on the dialects, where the target optimum is
near the source estimate; under collapse the optimum is unbounded, the quadratic approximation does not apply, and
shrinkage loses. Proposition~\ref{prop:survival}(a) rejects independence of score and label; it shows that some
ranking survives, not that the ranking is good enough to deploy. Part (b) tests the second question, but its
distribution-free bound is wide: with 12 items per class its half-width is 0.353. An asymptotic alternative uses the
DeLong variance of the AUROC on the logit scale \citep{qin2008comparison}, which is tighter but holds only
approximately with few labels.

\subsection{Procedure and evaluation}
\label{sec:triage-procedure}

The procedure has five steps. (1) Detect shift without labels with the domain classifier; if it fires, request $n$
randomly chosen target labels. (2) Test each candidate, the fine-tuned encoder and a language model, for a ranking
above chance (Proposition~\ref{prop:survival}a, Holm over candidates at $\delta = 0.05$). (3) If no candidate passes,
abstain: the labels give no evidence that either score is informative, and if neither is, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility} no recalibration, conformal set or confidence threshold is better than ignoring
the input. (4) Otherwise choose the passing candidate with the lowest log-loss on the labelled items after MAP BCTS and
predict with its MAP BCTS probabilities. (5) Certify a fixed score: in the \emph{Bonferroni} variant the conformal sets
and the FNR threshold use the chosen candidate's source-TS score on all $n$ items at level $\alpha/M$; in the
\emph{split} variant steps 2--4 use one half of the items and the sets use the resulting MAP BCTS score on the other
half at level $\alpha$. In the Bonferroni variant the point predictions therefore come from MAP BCTS and the sets from
source TS; for $K = 2$ the threshold on the source-TS score flags the same items as a threshold on any monotone
recalibration of it.

We run the procedure on every shifted condition with a target calibration split, for every converged base-encoder run
paired with each general language model and 20 resamples of $n \in \{25, 50, 100\}$ labels. The resamples are shared
across systems, so the shares below are shares of correlated resamples, not of independent deployments. With $n = 50$
on the original shifts the Bonferroni variant reaches macro-F1 0.794 (oracle choice 0.796, a router that reuses the
labels without correction 0.788, always the language model 0.715, the encoder with the same labels 0.682, the encoder
calibrated on the source 0.658) and lets test coverage fall below 0.85 in only 0.006 of deployments (reuse router
0.124, source-calibrated encoder 0.472; Table~\ref{tab:shift-triage-policies} in the appendix). Choosing by in-sample
log-loss is optimistic, so we also cross-fitted the choice: at $n = 50$ it picks the same system as the in-sample rule
in 0.959--0.979 of deployments on the original shifts and 0.884--0.945 on the natural targets, and mean macro-F1
changes by at most 0.015. Abstention in this study comes only from low power, since no target defeats both systems; a
stress test that destroys both rankings on a growing share of the items confirms that the gate then rejects at about
its nominal level (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-stress}).

Certifying a usable ranking costs more labels than detecting one. With 50 labels, the DeLong test certifies
AUROC above 0.65 for the offensive encoders in 0.738 of resamples and for the offensive language models in 0.664,
against 0.443 and 0.097 with the distribution-free bound; for sentiment the shares are 0.194 and 0.481 with DeLong and
zero with the bound. The label requirement of the survival test itself is planning guidance under a model of the
score distribution. On a balanced target, the test on all $n$ labels at the level of the first Holm step for two
candidates needs 25, 50, 75 and 300 labels for power 0.8 at AUROC 0.90, 0.80, 0.70 and 0.60, and the four score models we tried (binormal with
equal variances, with the positive class at half or twice the spread, and a shifted exponential) give the same
numbers. At a positive share of 0.1 the requirement grows and depends on the model: binormal with equal variances needs
200 labels at AUROC 0.70, whereas with the positive class at twice the spread it needs 300.

The false-negative certificate is the most expensive part. It needs 19 labelled positives for two candidates at
$\alpha = 0.1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}). On a balanced target 50 random labels contain them with probability
0.97; at 10\% prevalence 50 labels contain five positives on average and about 245 labels are needed for probability
0.9. Resampling the natural offensive targets to a positive share of 0.1 confirms this
(Table~\ref{tab:triage-prevalence-stress} in the appendix): the threshold flags every item in every deployment with 50
labels, in 0.35 of deployments with 200 and in none with 400, where the FNR is 0.034 at an FPR of 0.558. The point
predictions need far fewer labels: macro-F1 is 0.723 with 50 labels and 0.728 with 400. About 50 labels therefore
suffice to choose a system and correct its threshold, and several hundred are needed to certify it on a skewed target.
