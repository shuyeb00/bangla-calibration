\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-design}

\subsection{Source tasks}
Two Bangla source tasks carry the main grid. \textbf{Offensive language}: BD-SHS \citep{romim2022bdshs}, binary,
33{,}057 / 4{,}723 / 4{,}718 / 4{,}722 items for training / validation / calibration / test. \textbf{Sentiment}:
SentNoB \citep{islam2021sentnob}, three classes, 9{,}520 / 1{,}361 / 1{,}359 / 1{,}360 items. Two further source tasks
test whether the findings depend on the source language: \textbf{English OLID} \citep{zampieri2019olid} in the
TweetEval release \citep{barbieri2020tweeteval} (10{,}725 / 1{,}324 / 1{,}191 / 860; calibration split drawn from the
training split), and \textbf{SentiMix Hinglish} \citep{patwa2020sentimix} (12{,}480 / 2{,}998 / 1{,}500 / 3{,}000).
For BD-SHS, SentNoB and SentiMix Hinglish no two splits share an exact text; the TweetEval OLID splits share four to five texts between pairs of splits, which we keep as distributed.

\subsection{Target conditions}
Every target has a labelled calibration split (used only by the few-label methods and the oracle quantities) and a
disjoint test split.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Regional dialects of Bangla.} Three BIDWESH dialects (Barishal, Chittagong, Noakhali;
  calibration 610--611, test 2{,}443--2{,}445 items each) \citep{bidwesh} for the offensive task, and a Sylheti
  sentiment set (calibration 200, test 201) that we created by human translation of a uniform random sample of the
  SentNoB test split.
  \item \textbf{Natural Bangla--English code-mixing.} Code-mixed Chaos \citep{codemixedchaos}, a toxicity corpus in
  romanised Bangla (Banglish) with a row-aligned Bangla-script transliteration (calibration 2{,}035, test 8{,}144; toxic
  share 0.50), which gives a script intervention with identical content and labels; and MixTox-SA \citep{mixtox}
  (calibration 2{,}456, test 9{,}824; toxic share 0.34).
  \item \textbf{Tri-lingual code-mixing.} OffMix-3L and SentMix-3L \citep{goswami2023offmix} (Bangla--English--Hindi in
  three scripts; calibration 200 / 201, test 800 / 804).
  \item \textbf{Other languages.} SentiMix Hinglish and Spanglish \citep{patwa2020sentimix} as targets of the SentNoB
  models, SentNoB and Sylheti as targets of the Hinglish models, English OLID as a target of the BD-SHS models, and BD-SHS
  as a target of the OLID models.
\end{itemize}
Targets are never used for training, model selection or setting priors; the natural code-mixed and cross-language
targets were added after the original conditions and are treated as held out.

\subsection{Models}
\textbf{Encoders.} BanglaBERT \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, IndicBERT \citep{kakwani2020indicnlpsuite} and
XLM-R-base \citep{conneau2020xlmr}, each with full fine-tuning and LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} (rank 8, $\alpha = 32$,
query and value projections), five seeds each; BanglaBERT-large and XLM-R-large (full fine-tuning, learning rate
$10^{-5}$ with 10\% warm-up, five seeds); MuRIL \citep{khanuja2021muril} and mDeBERTa-v3-base \citep{he2023debertav3}
(full fine-tuning, five seeds). All encoders use inputs of up to 512 tokens, batch size 16, four epochs and the epoch with
the best validation macro-F1. The OLID and Hinglish source tasks use XLM-R-base, MuRIL and mDeBERTa-v3 with the recipes
of their BD-SHS runs. Runs that predict a single class or do not exceed the majority-class validation macro-F1 by
0.02 are reported as non-converged and excluded from aggregates (one LoRA run of the base grid).

\textbf{Instruction-tuned LLMs.} Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct \citep{qwen3}, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{llama3} and
Qwen3.5-9B, prompted zero-shot and few-shot (three demonstrations per class from the source training split, three
demonstration seeds). Class scores are the log-probabilities of the label words at the answer position, so every LLM
yields a score vector that is calibrated with the same methods as the encoders. Encoders and LLMs are compared on
identical item subsets (at most 1{,}500 items per split).

\subsection{Corrections, uncertainty sets and metrics}
Label-free corrections: source TS, vector and matrix scaling, isotonic regression, importance-weighted TS with a
character n-gram domain classifier, batch calibration and EM label-shift correction \citep{saerens2002adjusting}.
Few-label corrections with $n \in \{10, 25, 50, 100, \text{all}\}$ target labels (20 resamples, identical across
systems): maximum-likelihood and shrinkage TS, MAP BCTS, prior-aware batch calibration, last-layer retraining, few-label
fine-tuning, and in-context learning with target demonstrations. Uncertainty sets: split-conformal LAC sets at
$\alpha = 0.1$ and conformal risk control \citep{angelopoulos2024crc} of the false-negative rate of the offensive class
at 0.1, calibrated on the source split or on the $n$ target items. Metrics: accuracy, macro-F1, NLL, ECE (15 bins),
AUROC, coverage, set size, FNR and FPR, and selective-prediction curves.

\subsection{Statistical protocol}
Means are over converged seeds; contrasts between training choices use exact permutation or sign-flip tests with
bootstrap confidence intervals ($B = 10{,}000$ where stated), and Holm's procedure \citep{holm1979} within each
pre-defined family of contrasts. Interventional contrasts (script, label definition) were pre-registered with their
expected direction, and refuted predictions are reported.
