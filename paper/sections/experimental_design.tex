\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:experimental-design}

\paragraph{Source tasks.}
Two Bangla tasks carry the main grid: offensive-language detection on BD-SHS \citep{romim2022bdshs}, a hate-speech corpus with a
binary label (33{,}057 training items), and three-class sentiment on SentNoB \citep{islam2021sentnob} (9{,}520). Two further source
tasks test whether the findings depend on the source language: English OLID \citep{zampieri2019olid} in the TweetEval
release \citep{barbieri2020tweeteval}, and SentiMix Hinglish \citep{patwa2020sentimix}. Every source task has
training, validation, calibration and test splits (sizes in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data}).

\paragraph{Targets.}
Every target has a labelled calibration split, used only by the few-label methods and the oracle quantities, and a
disjoint test split.
\emph{Dialects}: the Barishal, Chittagong and Noakhali dialects of BIDWESH \citep{bidwesh}, three parallel translations
of the same BD-SHS sentences, for the offensive task; and Sylheti for sentiment, a set we created by human translation of a uniform random
sample of 401 SentNoB test items (calibration 200, test 201; Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data}).
\emph{Natural Bangla--English code-mixing}: Code-mixed Chaos \citep{codemixedchaos}, toxic and non-toxic social-media
comments in Banglish together with a row-aligned Bangla-script transliteration, which gives a script intervention with
near-identical content and identical labels; and MixTox-SA \citep{mixtox}, multilingual comments in native and romanised scripts, with
toxic against non-toxic and neutral as the binary label.
\emph{Tri-lingual code-mixing}: OffMix-3L \citep{goswami2023offmix} and SentMix-3L \citep{raihan2023sentmix},
Bangla--English--Hindi texts in three scripts written by students for the benchmark (construction details in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data}).
\emph{Other languages}: SentiMix Hinglish and Spanglish as targets of the SentNoB models, SentNoB and Sylheti as targets
of the Hinglish models, OLID as a target of the BD-SHS models and BD-SHS as a target of the OLID models.
The 21 shifted source--target pairs all have a labelled calibration split. The natural code-mixed and cross-language
targets were added after the original conditions and are held out: no prior, threshold or method choice is fitted on
them.

\paragraph{Encoders.}
BanglaBERT \citep{bhattacharjee2022banglabert}, IndicBERT \citep{kakwani2020indicnlpsuite} and XLM-R-base
\citep{conneau2020xlmr}, each fully fine-tuned and with LoRA \citep{hu2022lora}; BanglaBERT-large and XLM-R-large; MuRIL
\citep{khanuja2021muril} and mDeBERTa-v3-base \citep{he2023debertav3}; on BD-SHS and SentNoB, five seeds per
configuration. The OLID and Hinglish sources use XLM-R-base, MuRIL and mDeBERTa-v3 with the recipes of their BD-SHS runs.
Inputs are up to 512 tokens; the epoch with the best validation macro-F1 is kept. A run that predicts a single class or
does not exceed the majority-class validation macro-F1 by 0.02 is reported as non-converged and excluded from aggregates;
one LoRA run is affected (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-training}). The regime analysis uses every converged
cross-entropy run: 49 BD-SHS and 50 SentNoB runs of seven encoders, and 15 runs each for OLID and Hinglish.

\paragraph{LLMs.}
Qwen3-8B \citep{qwen3}, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 \citep{qwen34b2507}, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct \citep{llama3} and Qwen3.5-9B \citep{qwen35}, prompted zero-shot and
few-shot (three demonstrations per class from the source training split; three demonstration seeds). The class score is
the log-probability of each label word at the answer position, so each LLM yields a score vector that is recalibrated
with the same methods as the encoders, starting from its own source TS. LLMs and encoders are compared on identical items
(at most 1{,}500 per split). A fifth, Bangla-specific model (TigerLLM-1B; \citealp{raihan2025tigerllm}) did not follow the
answer format and is reported only in the appendix.

\paragraph{Methods and metrics.}
Label-free corrections: source TS, vector and matrix scaling, isotonic regression, importance-weighted TS with a
character n-gram domain classifier, batch calibration, and EM label-shift correction \citep{saerens2002adjusting}.
Few-label corrections with $n \in \{10, 25, 50, 100, \text{all}\}$ target labels and 20 resamples drawn identically for
every system: maximum-likelihood and shrinkage TS, MAP BCTS and prior-aware batch calibration; as label-spending
alternatives, last-layer retraining, few-label fine-tuning and in-context learning with target demonstrations.
Uncertainty sets: split-conformal LAC sets at $\alpha = 0.1$ and conformal risk control \citep{angelopoulos2024crc} of
the false-negative rate (FNR) of the offensive class at 0.1, calibrated on the source split or on the $n$ target items.
Metrics: macro-F1, negative log-likelihood (NLL), ECE with 15 bins, AUROC, coverage, set size, FNR, false-positive rate
(FPR), and selective risk.

\paragraph{Statistics.}
Means are over converged runs. Contrasts between training choices and between correction methods are tested with exact
permutation or sign-flip tests, with Holm's correction \citep{holm1979} within each pre-defined family of contrasts and
bootstrap confidence intervals over runs or items (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-statistics}). The expected outcomes
of the label-definition intervention, the prompt-definition variants and the cross-source regime map were written down
in our internal plan before those experiments were run (pre-specified, not publicly registered); refuted predictions
are reported.

\paragraph{Which runs a number refers to.}
Regime and correction numbers use every converged run on the full target test split. Comparisons with LLMs use the
runs of the three base encoders (29 BD-SHS, 30 SentNoB) on the LLM items, and the cross-definition test uses the 15 runs
of each source on the three shared architectures. The same pair therefore has slightly different means across
sections: BD-SHS models on OffMix-3L rank at 0.496, 0.481 and 0.519 respectively.
