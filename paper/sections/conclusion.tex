\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across the dialects, scripts, code-mixing and languages studied here, on two tasks centred on Bangla and with source
tasks in three languages, the common failure of fine-tuned classifiers was a misplaced decision threshold rather than
a wrong temperature. Temperature scaling recovers much of the lost log-loss but cannot move the threshold, however
many labels it is fitted on, and the temperature change one measures under such a shift is mostly a bias seen through
the wrong parameter. The bias is a shift of the scores, not of the class prior, so label-shift estimators recover the
wrong prior, and a bias correction must either assume a balanced target or take its prior from a few labels drawn from
the deployment stream. A collapse of the ranking was rare and, in the one case observed, tied to the target corpus
rather than to code-mixing. A few dozen target labels are enough to choose a system and correct its threshold; a
certificate on missed offensive posts costs hundreds when such posts are rare.
