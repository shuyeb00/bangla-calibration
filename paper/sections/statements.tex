\section*{Ethical Considerations}
\paragraph{Human participants.}
No external participants took part in this study. The Sylheti translations and all human labels used here, the blind
re-annotation of Sylheti items and the reading of OffMix-3L items, were produced by
two of the authors, who are native Sylheti and Bangla speakers, as part of their own research. No personal or
identifying information was collected, and no one was paid. Offensive examples are not reproduced in this article.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
The study concerns offensive-language detection, where miscalibrated decisions have uneven costs: missed offensive
posts harm their targets, and false flags silence speakers of under-served varieties. A moderation model can miss most
offensive posts in a new variety while remaining confident, and a popular label-free fix can make matters worse on
targets with few offensive posts. The triage procedure is meant to make such failures visible and to require a small
labelled check before deployment, but its guarantee on missed posts is paid for in false flags, which fall on the same
speakers.

\section*{Data and Code Availability}
The nine public datasets are used for research only, and no dataset text is redistributed with this article. Their
terms differ (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data}): SentiMix, BIDWESH, Code-mixed Chaos and MixTox-SA are released under
CC BY 4.0; BD-SHS, OffMix-3L and SentMix-3L carry a software licence on their repositories (MIT and AGPL-3.0) but no
separate data statement; SentNoB states no licence; and the offensive subset of TweetEval states none and remains
subject to the platform's terms. What is released is derived output from which no text can be recovered: the code,
every derived table, and the logits and item indices of every converged run and language-model system
(\zenodoRecord). Preparation scripts rebuild every split from the original releases except the Sylheti texts, which
are a translation of SentNoB items; we will release them if the SentNoB authors agree.

\section*{Use of Generative Models in the Research}
Two kinds of generative model are part of the methods and are reported as such. NLLB-200 3.3B translated the source
corpora of the factorial in Section~\ref{sec:results-factorial}, and four instruction-tuned language models are
evaluated as classifiers. Neither was used to produce or validate any label: every label in this study comes from the
original datasets or from the authors.

\section*{Compute}
All training and inference ran on NVIDIA T4 GPUs of free cloud notebooks. Training the 172 encoder runs on the
Bangla sources took 38.3 GPU-hours and their output passes 5.1 GPU-hours (peak memory 10.3 GB); the 60 runs of the
factorial took 16.0 GPU-hours of training, and the translations for it 4.5 GPU-hours. The time of the OLID and
Hinglish runs and of language-model inference was not recorded. Every analysis in this article runs on a CPU from the
released outputs.
