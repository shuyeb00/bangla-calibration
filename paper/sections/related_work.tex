\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Calibration under distribution shift.}
Modern networks are overconfident and temperature scaling is a strong post-hoc repair in domain
\citep{guo2017calibration}, but calibration degrades under shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}; for pre-trained transformers
\citet{desai2020calibration} found temperature scaling effective in and out of domain on English tasks, and
\citet{ahuja2022multilingual} report miscalibration of multilingual models in cross-lingual transfer. We add an analysis
that separates temperature, threshold and ranking failures and shows that the second dominates under language-variety
shift.

\paragraph{Label shift and bias correction.}
Prior adjustment by EM \citep{saerens2002adjusting}, black-box shift estimation \citep{lipton2018bbse} and bias-corrected
calibration \citep{alexandari2020maximum} address changes of the class prior with a fixed class-conditional
distribution, and posterior re-calibration interpolates towards a target prior \citep{tian2020posterior}. For prompted
LLMs, contextual calibration \citep{zhao2021calibrate} and batch calibration \citep{zhou2024batch} estimate a bias
without labels. Our targets violate the label-shift assumption, EM degenerates on them, and batch calibration works only
near a balanced target; a few labels make the bias correction safe.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction and risk control.}
Split conformal prediction and conformal risk control give finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability
\citep{angelopoulos2024crc}; weighted conformal prediction handles covariate shift with known density ratios
\citep{tibshirani2019weighted}, which our near-perfectly separable shifts do not admit. Choosing among models with the
same calibration data breaks the guarantee unless corrected \citep{liang2024conformal}; we use Bonferroni and
sample-splitting corrections.

\paragraph{Code-mixing, dialects and cross-dataset offensive-language detection.}
Code-mixed benchmarks \citep{khanuja2020gluecos, aguilar2020lince, patwa2020sentimix, goswami2023offmix} and dialect
resources document accuracy drops, and hate-speech detectors are known to generalise poorly across datasets partly
because definitions differ \citep{yin2021generalisable, fortuna2021generalize}. We show by intervention that such a
definition mismatch, not language coverage alone, produces the most severe calibration failure in our study.
