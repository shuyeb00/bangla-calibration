\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Calibration under distribution shift.}
Modern networks are overconfident, and temperature scaling is a strong post-hoc repair in domain
\citep{guo2017calibration}; calibration degrades under dataset shift \citep{ovadia2019trust}. For pre-trained
transformers, \citet{desai2020calibration} found temperature scaling effective in domain and label smoothing helpful
out of domain on English tasks, \citet{kong2020calibrated} regularise fine-tuning for calibration in and out of
distribution, and \citet{zhang2023transferable} transfer a post-hoc calibrator to noisy text.
\citet{ahuja2022multilingual} report miscalibration of multilingual models under cross-lingual transfer,
\citet{yang2026multilingualqa} study multilingual question answering, and \citet{roschewitz2025where} give the
image-classification counterpart of a large calibration study under dataset shift. Multi-domain temperature scaling
\citep{yu2022robust} adapts the temperature across labelled domains, and density-aware and distance-aware calibrators
use the model's hidden representations to temper confidence on unfamiliar inputs \citep{tomani2023density,
gasparin2024distance}. \citet{wang2026posthoc} estimate an out-of-domain temperature from unlabelled target data, and
\citet{dhar2026universal} learn a confidence calibrator for in-context classification that transfers across tasks and
language models. All of these act on the scale of the confidence; we compare a distance-aware calibrator in the
appendix, which, like temperature scaling, cannot change a decision. Our contribution to this line is
a decomposition of the failure into temperature, threshold and ranking components for dialect, script and code-mixing
shift, and the observation that the threshold component dominates there.

\paragraph{Label shift and bias correction.}
Prior adjustment by expectation maximisation \citep{saerens2002adjusting}, black-box and related shift estimators
\citep{lipton2018bbse, garg2020unified} and bias-corrected calibration \citep{alexandari2020maximum} address a change of
the class prior with fixed class-conditional distributions, and posterior recalibration interpolates towards a target
prior \citep{tian2020posterior}. For prompted language models, contextual calibration \citep{zhao2021calibrate}, domain-
context calibration \citep{fei2023mitigating}, batch calibration \citep{zhou2024batch} and task calibration
\citep{li2025task} estimate a bias without labels. The bias correction we find to be the right repair belongs to this family. What our targets add
is that the label-shift assumption fails on them: the predicted proportions move while the true ones barely do, the
required bias is mostly a shift of the score rather than of the prior, and the estimators built on the assumption
recover the wrong prior.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction and risk control.}
Split conformal prediction and conformal risk control give finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability
\citep{angelopoulos2024crc}, and can be reweighted for a known label shift \citep{podkopaev2021distribution} or carry
PAC guarantees under an estimated one \citep{si2024pac}. Weighted conformal prediction handles covariate shift with
known density ratios \citep{tibshirani2019weighted}; our sources and targets barely overlap, so there is little to
reweight. Choosing among models with the same calibration data breaks the guarantee unless corrected
\citep{liang2024conformal}; we use Bonferroni and sample-splitting corrections and report their cost in labels. For
language models, conformal coverage falls below its target when the prompt, the decoding temperature or the
quantisation changes \citep{zhu2026conformalized}; weighting calibration examples by their similarity to the test
prompt makes coverage more reliable under domain shift \citep{lin2026domainshift}. Abstention with deferral to human moderators has been built on
temperature-scaled confidence for offensive text under shift \citep{islam2026shiftaware}.

\paragraph{Code-mixing, dialects and cross-dataset offensive-language detection.}
Code-mixed benchmarks \citep{khanuja2020gluecos, aguilar2020lince, patwa2020sentimix, goswami2023offmix} evaluate models
on mixed-language text, and dialect transformations \citep{ziems2023multivalue} document accuracy drops on dialectal
English; \citet{joshi2025dialects} survey NLP for dialects more broadly. For Bangla, BIDWESH \citep{bidwesh} provides dialect translations of BD-SHS, and Code-mixed Chaos
\citep{codemixedchaos} and MixTox-SA \citep{mixtox} provide natural romanised and mixed-script text. Newer resources annotate Bangla--English code-mixing at the token
level \citep{afridi2026banglamix} and five regional dialects with romanised transliterations
\citep{jawad2026fivedialects}, and \citet{jawad2025benchmarking} annotate 600 YouTube comments in four dialects, Sylheti
among them, with binary sentiment. These are natural further targets for the procedure of
Section~\ref{sec:shift-triage}.
Hate-speech
detectors generalise poorly across datasets, in part because the datasets define their labels differently
\citep{fortuna2020toxic, yin2021generalisable, fortuna2021generalize}, and models trained on a dataset do not
necessarily reflect its stated definition \citep{khurana2025defverify}. Our source-corpus factorial is consistent with
this account on one student-written target and shows that the same contrast reverses on natural Banglish. Zero-shot
decisions of language models are sensitive to prompt wording \citep{seleznyov2025punctuation}; we observe the same for
their thresholds, not their rankings.
