\section{Proofs}
\label{sec:appendix-proofs}

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:impossibility}.}
Conditionally on $S = s$, the expected log-loss of $q = g(s)$ is the cross-entropy
$\mathbb{E}[-\log q_Y \mid S = s] = H(Q(\cdot \mid s)) + \mathrm{KL}(Q(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, q) \ge H(Q(\cdot \mid s))$,
with equality if and only if $q = Q(\cdot \mid s)$. Taking expectations over $S$ gives the bound. Under independence
$Q(\cdot \mid s) = \pi_Q$ for almost every $s$, so the bound equals $H_Q(Y)$ and is attained by the constant $\pi_Q$.
For the selective error, independence gives $Q(Y = h(S) \mid S) = \pi_Q(h(S)) \le \max_k \pi_Q(k)$ almost surely;
integrating over any $A \in \sigma(S)$ with $Q(A) > 0$ preserves the bound. $\square$

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:shrinkage}.}
With $n\hat L_n(\theta) \approx \mathrm{const} + \tfrac{n}{2}(\theta - \hat\theta_n)^\top F (\theta - \hat\theta_n)$, the
stationarity condition of the MAP objective is $nF(\theta - \hat\theta_n) + \Sigma^{-1}(\theta - \theta_P) = 0$, which
gives $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} = W_n\hat\theta_n + (I - W_n)\theta_P$ with $W_n = (nF + \Sigma^{-1})^{-1}nF$. In the
scalar case $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}} - \theta^* = w_n(\hat\theta_n - \theta^*) - (1 - w_n)\delta$, so with
$\mathbb{E}[\hat\theta_n - \theta^*] \approx 0$ and $\operatorname{Var}\hat\theta_n \approx \tau^2/n$,
$\mathrm{MSE}(\hat\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}}) = (1 - w_n)^2\delta^2 + w_n^2\tau^2/n$. This is below $\tau^2/n$ if and only if
$\delta^2 < \frac{\tau^2}{n}\frac{1 + w_n}{1 - w_n} = \frac{\tau^2}{n} + 2\sigma^2$. The source estimate $\theta_P$ is
treated as fixed, and the prior variance is a cross-task plug-in rather than an estimate from the target population.
$\square$

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:survival}.}
Under exchangeability and independence of score and label, the joint law of the scores given the labels is invariant
under permutations of the labels, so the observed AUROC and its permuted copies are exchangeable and the add-one Monte
Carlo p-value is valid \citep{phipson2010permutation}. The AUROC is a two-sample U-statistic with kernel in $[0, 1]$;
Hoeffding's inequality for U-statistics \citep{hoeffding1963} gives $\Pr(\hat A - A \ge t) \le \exp(-2mt^2)$, and the
bound follows. It also holds for sampling without replacement from a finite calibration pool, relative to the pool
AUROC. For three classes we use only the permutation test, applied to the macro one-vs-rest AUROC. $\square$

\paragraph{Proposition~\ref{prop:selection}.}
Each set calibrated at level $\alpha/M$ on exchangeable items with a fixed score covers with probability at least
$1 - \alpha/M$; the conformal quantile is finite when $\lceil (n+1)(1 - \alpha/M) \rceil \le n$, i.e.\
$n \ge M/\alpha - 1$. For any choice $\hat\jmath$, $\{Y_{n+1} \notin C_{\hat\jmath}\} \subseteq \bigcup_j \{Y_{n+1}
\notin C_j\}$, and the union bound gives miscoverage at most $\alpha$. For risk control with losses in $[0, 1]$,
$L_{\hat\jmath} \le \sum_j L_j$ pointwise, so $\mathbb{E}[L_{\hat\jmath}] \le M \cdot \alpha/M$. For the false-negative
rate, conformal risk control on the $n_1$ positive items selects the largest threshold $\lambda$ with
$(n_1 \widehat{\mathrm{FNR}}(\lambda) + 1)/(n_1 + 1) \le \alpha/M$; a threshold that misses no calibration positive
satisfies this only if $1/(n_1 + 1) \le \alpha/M$, i.e.\ $n_1 \ge M/\alpha - 1$, and otherwise every item is flagged.
With sample splitting the chosen candidate is independent of the calibration part, and the single-candidate guarantee
applies conditionally on the choice. When the same items also decide whether to deploy at all, the bound applies to the
joint event of deploying and missing; conditionally on deployment it is $\alpha / \Pr(\text{deploy})$. $\square$

\section{Numerical Checks of the Propositions}
\label{sec:appendix-simulation}
We checked the propositions by simulation, using the implementations of the analyses. With a score independent of the
label (class prior 0.52 and 0.48) and 200 labelled items, temperature scaling, BCTS and isotonic regression never beat
the constant predictor on 20{,}000 fresh items beyond Monte Carlo error (smallest excess log-loss $-0.0002$ nats over 100
replications), and the selective error at 80\% coverage is 0.501 against the bound of 0.48; with an informative score
BCTS beats the constant by 0.215 nats. In the Gaussian model of Proposition~\ref{prop:shrinkage}, the MAP estimate has
the lower Monte Carlo mean squared error in exactly those of the six settings tested in which
$\delta^2 < 2\sigma^2 + \tau^2/n$. The permutation test of Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} rejects a true null in 0.0485--0.0510 of 2{,}000
replications at $\delta = 0.05$, and the Hoeffding bound covered the true AUROC in every replication; for a weak
ranking (AUROC 0.69) the test has power 0.49 with 12 and 0.78 with 25 items per class, which is why weak rankings need
about 100 labels when half of them are held out. Choosing between two equally good systems on 50 labelled items and
calibrating the chosen system's set on the same items at $\alpha = 0.1$ gives mean coverage 0.886, with coverage below
0.85 in 0.189 of deployments; calibrating at $\alpha/2$ gives 0.952 and 0.0055, and sample splitting 0.924 and 0.089.
The false-negative threshold flags every item exactly when the number of positives is below $M/\alpha - 1$, for one
and for two candidates.

\paragraph{Splits and preprocessing.}
Table~\ref{tab:data} lists every split. BD-SHS (33{,}057 training and 4{,}723 validation items) and SentNoB (9{,}520 and
1{,}361) were split once into training, validation, calibration and test sets with no shared exact text; OLID uses the
TweetEval training (10{,}725 after removing the calibration split), validation (1{,}324) and test splits; the Hinglish
source uses the official SentiMix training split without texts that occur in the development or test sets (12{,}480),
the development split for validation (2{,}998) and the official test split. Bangla texts are cleaned by URL removal and
whitespace normalisation followed by the normaliser required by BanglaBERT; other texts are normalised with NFKC. The
natural code-mixed corpora are deduplicated (conflicting duplicates are dropped) and split 20/80 into calibration and
test, stratified by label; the two scripts of Code-mixed Chaos share one split (10{,}179 of its 10{,}234 rows remain).
For SentiMix Spanglish, whose official
test labels are not distributed, the official development split is the test split and the calibration split is drawn
from the training split, so the two come from different releases. Of the BIDWESH test items, 8.6\% are translations of
BD-SHS training sentences; removing them changes macro-F1 by at most 0.0053 and AUROC by at most 0.0033 in any run.

\paragraph{How the targets were built.}
These facts limit what each target can show. BIDWESH renders the same BD-SHS sentences in three dialects, so its three
targets are not independent. Code-mixed Chaos is balanced (5{,}117 toxic, 5{,}117 non-toxic) by adding non-toxic comments
from a public emotion corpus, so its label is partly tied to the source corpus. MixTox-SA has three classes; we count
neutral as non-toxic, which gives the toxic share of 0.342. OffMix-3L was written by ten undergraduate students;
three annotators agreed on 1{,}106 of 1{,}734 items (raw agreement 63.7\%), only those were kept, and a second round left
1{,}001 items, so it contains unusually clear-cut items.

\paragraph{The Sylheti test set.}
We drew 760 items uniformly at random from the SentNoB test split; two of the authors, both native Sylheti speakers,
translated the first 401 of them in the random order into Sylheti written in Bangla script, keeping the source's
non-standard spelling where possible. Each translation was made by one of them. The 401 items do not differ from the
untranslated remainder in label distribution or length. They are split by label into 200 calibration and 201 test items.
To check label validity without relying on the translators' own judgement of the source, both labelled a
stratified sample of 100 Sylheti items without seeing the gold label. Their agreement with the SentNoB gold label was
0.650 and 0.616 (Cohen's $\kappa$ 0.498 and 0.422), against 0.483 and 0.533 for the same annotators on a blind sample of
SentNoB's own test items; the inter-annotator $\kappa$ was 0.589. The disagreement concentrates at the boundary between
positive and neutral, as in the source data. The Sylheti set is a derivative of SentNoB, whose repository states no
licence, so its texts are not redistributed; we will release them if the SentNoB authors agree.

\section{Models, Training and Prompting}
\label{sec:appendix-training}

\paragraph{Encoders.}
Full fine-tuning uses learning rate $2 \cdot 10^{-5}$, batch size 16, weight decay 0.01 and four epochs for the
base-size encoders; mDeBERTa-v3 adds 10\% linear warm-up, and BanglaBERT-large and XLM-R-large use learning rate
$10^{-5}$ with 10\% warm-up, a standard large-encoder recipe chosen before training. LoRA uses rank 8, $\alpha = 32$ and
dropout 0.1 on the query and value projections, with the learning rate selected per task and model from a seed-42
validation sweep. Large encoders and mDeBERTa-v3 use gradient checkpointing; we never use gradient accumulation. Inputs
are up to 512 tokens with dynamic padding. IndicBERT's default fast tokenizer strips every Bangla non-spacing mark,
whereas its pretraining normalisation removes only the hasanta and the nukta; we reproduce the pretraining
normalisation before tokenisation.

\paragraph{Convergence.}
One of the 60 base-grid runs (IndicBERT with LoRA, offensive, one seed) did not converge by the rule fixed before training and
is excluded; the configuration is reported over four seeds. One label-smoothing run did not converge. Under the
base-model recipe, three of six XLM-R-large runs collapsed to a single class; these runs are reported only as a recipe
sensitivity check, and the large-encoder results use the large-encoder recipe, under which all 20 runs converged. All
architecture, OLID and Hinglish runs converged.

\paragraph{LLMs.}
Each prompt contains an English instruction, the text and a one-word answer format. For offensive language the
instruction asks whether the text ``contains hate speech, insults, abuse, threats, or vulgar language''; for sentiment it
asks for the sentiment that the text expresses. The class score is the log-probability of the label word at the answer
position; every label word is one token for every model, so the scores carry no length bias. The probability of
producing any label is recorded. Thinking
is disabled through the chat template for Qwen3 and Qwen3.5. The four
general LLMs place at least 0.97 of their probability on a label word on average on every split of the original
conditions; the exception among the held-out targets is Llama-3.1-8B on Spanglish. TigerLLM-1B, a Bangla-specific
instruction-tuned model, did not follow the format in the few-shot setting and its zero-shot accuracy was at chance;
it is excluded from the comparisons. Few-shot demonstrations are three per class from the source training split, with
20--150 characters, for three demonstration seeds.

\section{Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:appendix-statistics}

Contrasts between paired configurations (training objectives, recalibration methods) use exact sign-flip tests over
seeds or Monte Carlo sign-flip tests over runs; unpaired configurations (scale, architectures) use exact permutation
tests. Holm's procedure is applied within each pre-defined family (Table~\ref{tab:contrast-families-holm}). Confidence
intervals use the bootstrap with 10{,}000 resamples, hierarchical over configurations and then seeds where runs are
nested, and paired over items for comparisons on identical items (2{,}000 resamples for item-level differences of
AUROC). Few-label methods use 20 resamples of the labelled target items, drawn with a fixed seed per $(n,
\text{resample})$ so that every system sees the same items. Five-versus-five permutation tests have a smallest
attainable $p$ of $2/252$, so a family of 120 such tests cannot reach significance after correction; we report it
descriptively. In the two LLM recalibration families the sign-flip test treats the 16 systems (four LLMs, four
prompting settings) as exchangeable, although they come from four models; a test over the four models alone could not
go below $p = 1/8$, so these families describe these models rather than a population of LLMs. The hierarchical
bootstrap intervals, which
resample models, exclude zero for all 16 significant contrasts on the original targets but for only 24 of the 26 on the
natural targets; the two exceptions are NLL contrasts on MixTox-SA with 25 labels. Intervals of the cross-definition
contrasts are over items; variation across seeds and architectures enters through the stratified permutation test.

Shrinkage TS and MAP BCTS use Gaussian priors on $\log T$ and on each free bias, with $b_0 = 0$ for identifiability. The
variances are empirical-Bayes estimates from the other task: for encoders, on $\log T$ and the bias, 0.433 and 1.24 for
offensive language and 1.55 and 0.063 for sentiment. Refitting MAP BCTS with 50 labels with these variances scaled by
0.25 or 4, or with unit variance, changes mean macro-F1 over the targets by at most 0.01 for encoders and for LLMs, and on
single targets by $-0.041$ to $+0.048$.

\subsection{Label-free recalibrators and label-shift correction}
\label{sec:appendix-recalibration}
Vector and matrix scaling lower NLL slightly in domain and on the milder shifts, and by 0.036 and 0.041 on SentMix-3L.
Isotonic regression lowers ECE but raises the NLL on SentMix-3L by 0.211 on average, because out-of-range scores fall
into bins with near-zero probability. Importance-weighted TS changes NLL by at most 0.0073: a character n-gram domain
classifier separates source and target with AUROC 0.976--1.000, so there is almost no overlap to reweight. EM estimation
of the target class prior \citep{saerens2002adjusting} on top of bias-corrected TS lowers macro-F1 on every code-mixed and
cross-language target, for encoders and LLMs alike; on OffMix-3L it drives the estimated offensive prior to zero for
every system (macro-F1 0.343). It is harmless on the dialects. EM assumes that the class-conditional distributions are
unchanged, which these targets violate.

\subsection{Conformal sets under shift}
Split-conformal sets calibrated on the source split ($\alpha = 0.1$) lose coverage on the shifted targets: for the base
encoders, 0.799 on Chittagong, 0.520 on OffMix-3L and 0.750 on SentMix-3L, against 0.901 and 0.911 in domain. Calibrating
on 50 target items restores coverage of 0.883--0.930 in every shifted pair of the regime map.

\subsection{Artifacts of OffMix-3L}
\label{sec:appendix-artifacts}
OffMix-3L was written by students for the benchmark. Clustering the unlabeled texts by character n-grams gives clusters
that are almost pure in the label: the adjusted mutual information between cluster and label is 0.47--0.81 for 5 to 50
clusters, against 0.19--0.28 on BD-SHS test. A character n-gram classifier reaches AUROC 0.938, 0.976 and 0.993 with 25,
50 and 100 OffMix-3L training items; from 50 items on it ranks better than every LLM. We therefore evaluate rankings within clusters: with five clusters
the zero-shot LLMs reach 0.910--0.950, the BD-SHS encoders 0.470 on average and the OLID-trained encoders 0.784--0.885 by
architecture. On the natural code-mixed targets the same audit finds much weaker artifacts (Code-mixed Chaos at most
0.37 adjusted mutual information, MixTox-SA at most 0.083).

\subsection{Human reading of the OffMix-3L labels}
Before the cross-definition experiment, two of the authors relabelled 100 random OffMix-3L test items without seeing the
gold label, using an insult-based definition that matches BD-SHS. Neither reads Hindi in Devanagari, and one marked 61
items as not judgeable. Their agreement with the gold label was at chance (0.44 and
0.41, $\kappa$ 0.015 and 0.078), against 0.825 and 0.848 on BD-SHS test items. With complete English translations of 100
further items, one annotator recovered part of the gold label (agreement 0.677, $\kappa$ 0.359), the other applied the
insult-based definition and labelled almost every item non-offensive; the two annotators agreed with each other at
$\kappa = 0.030$. We report this round as consistent with, but much weaker than, the cross-definition intervention of
Section~\ref{sec:results-causes}, which does not depend on our judgement. Chance agreement also fits noisy labels, not
only a different definition.

\subsection{Contamination}
\label{sec:appendix-contamination}
For 250 items of each test set we compared guided completion, in which the prompt names the data set and split, with
general completion of the same item from its first part \citep{golchin2024time}, scoring the completions by ROUGE-L
against the true continuation. No model produced a near-verbatim completion of any
item, and an exact five-word continuation occurred for at most 0.004 of the items. Llama-3.1-8B completed every set
worse under the guided prompt than under the general one, yet ranks OffMix-3L as well as the other LLMs. Guided gains of
Qwen3-4B on OffMix-3L (0.025) and SentMix-3L (0.027) equal its gain on our unpublished Sylheti set (0.027), so they
reflect the prompt rather than membership. The largest effects above the Sylheti control (Qwen3-8B on SentMix-3L and
Banglish Chaos, ROUGE-L differences 0.012 and 0.014) are small and contain no verbatim recall.

\subsection{Prompt definitions}
Rewriting the offensive instruction with the OLID annotation guideline left the OffMix-3L ranking and threshold almost
unchanged (AUROC 0.916 against 0.913; 6.7\% predicted offensive against 47.8\% in the gold labels), contrary to our
prediction. A broader definition that also counts hostile or harshly critical remarks raised OffMix-3L AUROC by 0.040 for
all four LLMs and lowered BD-SHS AUROC by 0.027 for all four, a sign crossover consistent with the label-definition
account. A paraphrase of the default instruction changed macro-F1 by $-0.118$ to $+0.092$ per model and target, so
zero-shot thresholds are sensitive to wording while rankings are not. The sentiment prompt calls the text Bangla;
removing that statement did not help on Hinglish or Spanglish (macro-F1 changes of $-0.011$ and $-0.018$).


\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:appendix-reproducibility}

The released code reproduces the study at three levels. The supplement carries the anonymised model outputs. (1) From the released tables, a single script rebuilds every
figure, every table and every contrast statistic in this paper and runs the unit tests in about two minutes on a CPU;
a clean run reproduces the released files byte for byte, and a test checks every number quoted in the text against
those tables. (2) From the saved model outputs (the logits and item indices of every encoder run and LLM system), the
same script regenerates every table of results on a CPU, given the prepared datasets; while the Sylheti texts are
unreleased, the conditions that need them stay at level 1. (3) From the public datasets, the training and inference
scripts rebuild the models on two T4 GPUs. Every run records its seed, code version, library versions and an input
fingerprint. Dataset texts are not redistributed; preparation scripts rebuild the splits from the original releases.
The unit tests verify the statistical components against their definitions or against simulation: conformal coverage,
conformal risk control and its minimum number of positives, bias-corrected temperature scaling, batch calibration and
its prior-aware variant, the excess area under the risk--coverage curve, the permutation test and Holm's procedure, and the shrinkage condition of
Proposition~\ref{prop:shrinkage}.
