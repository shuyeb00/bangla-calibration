\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What to do in practice.}
Detect shift with a domain classifier, not with the model's confidence. After a detected shift, correct the class bias
rather than only the temperature. Without labels, batch calibration is appropriate only if the target classes are
believed to be roughly balanced; otherwise about 50 labels suffice to estimate the prior or to fit MAP BCTS. The same 50
labels test whether the ranking survived, choose between the fine-tuned encoder and an LLM, and certify coverage and
the false-negative rate. Fine-tuning on the target becomes the better use of labels only at a few hundred labels.

\paragraph{Why thresholds move.}
Our evidence locates the failure but does not explain why the threshold moves. One plausible account is that
text the encoder cannot read well receives scores close to those of bland in-domain text, which pushes its predictions
towards the non-offensive class. The script intervention is consistent with this account: the BD-SHS encoders label 16.4\% of the Banglish Chaos comments as toxic and
26.1\% of the same comments in Bangla script, against 50\% in the gold labels. A mechanistic test is left for future
work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Scope.} Two classification tasks; the main grid is Bangla, and the English and Hindi--English sources use
  three architectures each, against seven for the Bangla sources. Encoders have up to 560M parameters and LLMs up to 9B;
  generative tasks and proprietary models are not covered.
  \item \emph{Regimes.} The regime names and the reading rule were fixed in advance, but the operational thresholds were
  set after inspecting the results. The conclusion that matters for practice does not depend on where they are drawn:
  in each of the 16 pairs whose predicted marginal moves by more than 0.10, label-free bias correction raises macro-F1
  in at least 93\% of runs, while temperature scaling changes no decision anywhere.
  \item \emph{Data.} The Sylheti set is small and was translated by the authors; a blind re-annotation matched the gold
  labels about as well as the same annotators matched SentNoB in domain (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-data}). OffMix-3L
  carries strong writing-style artifacts, so we rely on the natural code-mixed targets for claims about code-mixing. The
  Spanglish calibration and test splits come from different releases, so their exchangeability is not guaranteed.
  \item \emph{Statistics.} Many results are descriptive means over runs. Formal tests with multiplicity control are
  reported for pre-defined families; five-versus-five comparisons cannot reach significance after correction in large
  families and are reported descriptively.
  \item \emph{Guarantees} are marginal over target calibration and test items and assume exchangeability within the
  target. When the same items also decide whether to deploy, the coverage bound holds jointly with the deployment event.
  \item \emph{Contamination.} No item-level memorisation was detected, but exposure of the LLMs to the public test sets
  cannot be excluded.
\end{itemize}
